Early Stage Testing

The Goal: We tested the CCP Supervisor feature prototype with 10 users in supervisory roles (ANC, PNC, SNCU). These users regularly oversee nurses' actions in the CCP and ensure sessions are conducted consistently. The goal was to collect feedback on the feature's strengths, weaknesses, usability, and areas for improvement, with a focus on user needs.

Usability testing Observations

We shared a clickable prototype to the nurses and supervisors and asked them to interact with it. During this process, we asked various questions to understand how they perceived the feature and assess its usability.

Also, we reminded them that we were just testing and had no commitments yet.

Pre-Test Questions

User's Background:

  • Name, demographic details, designation, and tasks: What is your name, demographic information & designation? What are the key tasks you're responsible for at your facility and within the CCP?
  • Current task management: How are you currently accomplishing these tasks?
  • Experience with HEP app: Have you used the HEP app before? If so, how has your experience been so far?

Setting the Context

"Imagine you're Aparna, a supervisor in the ANC department, overseeing four nurses. Your task is to track their CCP-related activities"

To do this, you launch the HEP app and see this screen.

Testing the Prototype

Home Tab:

  • What are your initial thoughts as you view this screen? What do you think it's trying to communicate?
  • What do you expect to happen when you tap "Number of Sessions" or "View Nurses"?
  • Can you explain what these graphs (pointing to sessions and participants) are telling you?
  • What do you understand by "Last marked session," and how important is this information for you as a supervisor?

Sessions Tab:

Everyone's Sessions:

  • Whose sessions do you think are listed on this screen?
  • What do you think the red, green, and yellow bars represent?
  • How would you go about viewing the details of one of the sessions listed here?
  • If you conduct a session, where do you think it would appear on this page?
  • Do you think there's a way to edit a session's details? If yes, how would you do it?

Mark Tab : Could you please demonstrate how you would mark attendance for a CCP session that you have conducted?

Learning Tab:

  • We've provided CCP tools on the app, such as flipcharts, rollers, and training manuals. Where do you think they might be located?
  • When you arrive at the Learning section, can you quickly tell us what you notice on this screen?
  • What do you think the "Courses" section is referring to?
  • Could you open a flipchart for us, please?

Notification :

  • Can you try opening the bell icon and tell us what you see inside? What do you infer from the following:
  • Refresher training
  • New nurse added (What does this inform you?)
  • Duplicate Session Alert (What do you understand by this, and what action could be taken?)
  • You have been tagged (What could this mean?)
  • QAT Report
  • New Course Added

Similarly, can you tell us what may be inside the profile icon ?

Post Test Questions

Overall Impression:

  • How easy was the app to use?
  • How often would you use the app?

Pain Points:

  • Any frustrations or challenges?
  • Were you unsure of what to do next?

Favourite/Least Favourite Features:

  • What did you like most?
  • Any features you didn't like?

Improvements:

  • What would you improve?
  • What hinders you as a supervisor?

RBAC features

  • Nurse: Conducts sessions for caregivers, providing guidance and support. They conduct sessions only for one department (ANC, PNC, SNCU)
  • Implementation Team: Oversees multiple facilities and department supervisors within a district. They track sessions, attend nurse-led sessions, and provide feedback.
  • Supervisor: Manages nurses within a specific department and may also conduct sessions as needed.
  • Monitoring Team: Oversees multiple facilities across districts. They assess session quality & provide feedback to ensure high standards in nurse-led sessions.

Learnings

This experience deepened my understanding of designing for impact-driven, non-tech-savvy users while ensuring accessibility and engagement.

Platform Design:

  • Engagement & Efficiency – Keeping nurses motivated while streamlining their workflow
  • User-Centric Design – Creating intuitive experiences for non-tech-savvy health workers
  • Multilingual Support – Building inclusive, accessible interfaces across regional languages

Overall Learnings:

  • Human-centered design is essential, focusing on ease of use, minimal cognitive load, and clear workflows for frontline healthcare workers
  • Technology adoption varies; gradual onboarding, guided interactions, and offline solutions are necessary
  • Multilingual UI design needs cultural context, readability, and layout adjustments beyond simple translation
