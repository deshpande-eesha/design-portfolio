Overview

What does Procore do?

Procore Technologies is a construction management software platform helping professionals manage projects, resources and financials from early planning through closeout.

What is Portfolio Management?

It is the process of selecting, prioritizing and overseeing a collection of construction projects to ensure they align with an organization's goals, optimize resource utilization, control risks and maximize performance.

It enables decisions about which projects to pursue, allocation of budgets and balancing outcomes.

Current user challenges

Most owners handled this critical planning phase outside Procore, using Excel or Google Sheets to track ideas, budgets and forecasts. These tools offered flexibility but came with major challenges.

Version control issues - capital planning information was scattered across Excel sheets, email threads and folders with conflicting data.

Manual and error-prone prioritization - strategic alignment and risk scoring were inconsistent across teams, with broken formulas leading to incorrect prioritization unnoticed.

No integration with active project execution already happening in Procore.

No role-based access for sensitive financial data, and limited collaboration made it difficult to share updates across departments.

Scoring mechanism

A scorecard framework measures project performance across financial, customer, internal process and learning perspectives - converting high-level strategic goals into actionable, measurable KPIs (like safety, cost, time and quality) to improve decision-making and ensure portfolio success.

Strategic Parameters
Strategic Fit Score

A qualitative or quantitative rating reflecting how a project contributes to specific, defined strategic objectives.

Budget vs. Importance

A comparison of the percentage of the portfolio budget invested in a specific driver against that driver’s perceived importance.

Business alignment

An assessment of a project’s expected outcomes against the organization’s SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) goals.

Financial Parameters
Net Present Value (NPV)

Difference between the present value of all future cash inflows and outflows, indicating if a project is profitable after accounting for the time value.

Return on Investment

A percentage metric quantifying the financial gain or loss generated by a project relative to its total cost.

Profitability Index (PI)

Ratio comparing the present value of future cash inflows to the initial investment, where a value greater than 1 indicates profitability.

Payback Period

The length of time required for a project’s cumulative cash inflows to fully recover the initial investment cost.

Risk Parameters
Risk Score

Quantitative score derived by multiplying the likelihood of a risk occurring by the severity of its potential impact.

Risk Exposure

Comprehensive measure of the total potential impact of all identified risks combined.

Resource Availability

Assessment of the readiness and presence of required skilled labor, equipment, and technology for execution.

Technical Feasibility

Evaluation of whether the technical infrastructure and expertise are currently in place or can be acquired.

Qualitative Parameters
Urgency

Rating reflecting the immediate need for a project to capitalize on opportunities or mitigate immediate threats.

Health and Safety Benefit

Rating evaluating the project’s ability to provide immediate or long-term improvements to public or employee safety.

Environmental Impact

Measure of whether the project will result in quantifiable improvements to air/water quality or reductions in greenhouse gas emissions.

Equity Impact

Determination of whether the project addresses outcome disparities based on demographics and includes community engagement.

KPI metric

We gave all projects different KPIs around schedule and budget for scoring.

Reflections and learnings

Four principles that shaped every decision in this project.

Simplify complex financials

Owners care most about budget health. The design must make millions of dollars easy to track at a glance.

  • Balancing scale: displayed large data sets (millions in budget) alongside minute details (individual project status) without overwhelming the user
  • Data hierarchy: organized screens to show big numbers first, with the option to dive into project details later
  • Visualizing utilization: used progress-based UI elements to communicate how much of a capital plan is actually "at work" versus sitting idle

Support smarter decisions

Owners need tools to help them choose the right projects, not just store a list of them.

  • Standardized scoring: implemented scoring frameworks (strategic alignment, risk, financial return) to remove subjectivity from project intake
  • Scenario modeling: designed "what-if" workflows that let owners simulate how different plan combinations impact total portfolio risk
  • Prioritization frameworks: developed interfaces that surface the most critical projects based on composite ratings, ensuring impactful work is funded first

Manage long-term planning

Construction cycles last years. The UI needs to handle current work and future ideas simultaneously.

  • Plan statuses: clearly separated "concept" projects for the future from "active" projects happening now
  • Multi-year views: simplified navigation so users can jump between different budget years without getting lost
  • Centralized intake: created a standard way to add projects so all proposals look the same for easy comparison

Optimize for the busy executive

Owners have little time. The interface must be presentation-ready and easy to navigate.

  • Scannable layouts: used clean tables and status tags so users can understand project health in seconds
  • Reduced friction: simplified "Add Project" forms to capture only the most essential data first
  • Action-oriented UI: placed primary buttons (like "Build Scenario") in high-visibility areas to speed up the workflow
