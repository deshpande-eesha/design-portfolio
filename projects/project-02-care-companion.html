Overview

Noora Health's mission is to -

improve health outcomes by training family caregivers to support their loved ones, turning hospital spaces into classrooms

What is the Care Companion Program?

Streamlines caregiver training in hospitals across India, Nepal, Bangladesh and Indonesia, enabling nurses, supervisors and implementation teams to manage, track and monitor caregiver sessions

Identification & Prioritization

Family members are identified as caregivers, focusing on public hospitals to support marginalized populations

Healthcare Staff Training

Nurses are trained to teach caregivers caregiving skills using structured methods

Family Caregiver Training

Caregivers receive on-site training with videos and flipcharts. Nurses track sessions using a mobile app

Ongoing Support

Caregivers get reference materials and post-discharge support via WhatsApp enhancing communication

How were Nurses tracking the sessions before?

Nurses in government hospitals faced challenges with paper based tracking - missing records, offline issues and time-consuming data entry. A simple mobile app was introduced to ease tracking: nurses fitting this in between patients, often on a shared phone and a weak connection.

Previously using - Pen and paper

Using pen and paper is slow, error-prone, and hard to organize. Records can be lost, photos can't be stored, and manual data entry takes time. It also lacks real-time updates and isn't eco-friendly.

Improved experience with Mobile App

Using a mobile app makes session tracking faster and more reliable. It stores photos, works offline, updates in real time, and reduces manual effort.

My role

Designing the Care Companion Program (HEP) for nurses, supervisors, implementation, and monitoring teams - enabling government hospital staff across India, Nepal, Bangladesh, and Indonesia to:

Mark training sessions conducted for caregivers and upload supporting photos

Access a learning section with educational materials for reference

Review past sessions to track their progress

Manage profiles with essential details

Multi-language interface to support diverse linguistic backgrounds

Role-Based Access Control for a seamless, nurse-focused experience

Early Stage Testing

The Goal: We tested the CCP Supervisor feature prototype with 10 users in supervisory roles (ANC, PNC, SNCU). These users regularly oversee nurses' actions in the CCP and ensure sessions are conducted consistently. The goal was to collect feedback on the feature's strengths, weaknesses, usability, and areas for improvement, with a focus on user needs.

Usability testing Observations

We shared a clickable prototype to the nurses and supervisors and asked them to interact with it. During this process, we asked various questions to understand how they perceived the feature and assess its usability.

Also, we reminded them that we were just testing and had no commitments yet.

Pre-Test Questions

User's Background:

  • Name, demographic details, designation, and tasks: What is your name, demographic information & designation? What are the key tasks you're responsible for at your facility and within the CCP?

Home Tab:

Sessions Tab: Everyone's Sessions:

Mark Tab:

Login

  • Personalized Interface: Users select country and language
  • Secure Access: OTP verification protects accounts
  • Informed Consent: Users review privacy policy before continuing

Onboarding

  • Quick Check: Nurses confirm details before proceeding
  • Auto-Filled Info: Reduces manual entry for accuracy
  • Instant Start: Save profile to begin CCP journey

Dashboard

  • Warm Welcome – Engages users with a personalized greeting
  • Dept Snapshot – 30-day session and participant trends
  • Key Metrics – Month-over-month performance changes
  • Easy Navigation – Quick links to nurses, sessions, and participants
  • Latest Session – Highlights recent session for transparency

Notifications

The notification system ensures critical updates reach users efficiently, supporting quick decisions, better coordination and improved patient care

Subtext adds context, helping users understand the notification's purpose and prompting timely action for a smoother experience

  • Training Alerts – Refresher courses and learning updates
  • Team Changes – New nurse notifications for smooth onboarding
  • Ops Alerts – Duplicate session warnings and tagging tools
  • Quality Checks – Audit reports and performance insights

Keeping nurses updated

  • Refresher Alerts – Help nurses reinforce skills and align with best practices
  • Academy Updates – Notify nurses of new courses to support continuous learning

Nurse list

Supervisor Tools – Simplified oversight of CCP enrollment and training:

  • Invite Nurses – Reach out to those not yet enrolled
  • Active Team – View current ANC, PNC, and SNCU nurses
  • History Access – Review past nurses and sessions
  • Track Invites – Monitor pending enrollments
  • Profile Insights – See nurse photos, sessions, participants, and last activity

Error Prevention

To maintain accurate training records, the system detects potential duplicate sessions when more than one nurse marks the same session in the app.

  • Duplicate Detection – Flags sessions with matching details
  • Supervisor Alert – Notifies for review and action
  • Decision Required – Confirm as duplicate or valid entry
  • Justification Needed – Provide reason if not marked duplicate

Marking a session

  • Date & Time – Select session date and shift
  • Conducted By – Automatically records the nurse leading the session
  • Session Type & Location – Specify the session category and venue (e.g., ANC waiting area)
  • Participants Trained – Enter the number of patients and caregivers trained
  • Upload Images – Add up to three images for documentation

Nurse profile

A clear view of nurse details, training impact, and session history:

  • Profile – Name, state, facility, role, department, and training date
  • Overview – Total sessions, participants, mobile follow-ups, and 30-day graph
  • Session List – Log of sessions with dates, participant counts, and markings

Learning

  • Flipcharts – Visual aids on child health, antenatal, and postnatal care
  • Manuals – Best practice guides and procedures
  • Courses – Modules for professional growth and upskilling

Session details

Session Marking – Ensures accurate records and transparent impact tracking:

  • Consistent Records – Input date, type, location, and shift
  • Auto-Logged Facilitator – System captures nurse details
  • Impact Metrics – Track patients and caregivers reached
  • Training Proof – Upload up to three supporting images

RBAC features

Nurse

Conducts sessions for caregivers, providing guidance and support. They conduct sessions only for one department (ANC, PNC, SNCU)

Implementation Team

Oversees multiple facilities and department supervisors within a district. They track sessions, attend nurse-led sessions, and provide feedback.

Supervisor

Manages nurses within a specific department and may also conduct sessions as needed.

Monitoring Team

Oversees multiple facilities across districts. They assess session quality & provide feedback to ensure high standards in nurse-led sessions.

Learnings

This experience deepened my understanding of designing for nurses fitting this in between patients, often on a shared phone and a weak connection, while ensuring accessibility and engagement.

Platform Design:

  • Engagement & Efficiency – Keeping nurses motivated while streamlining their workflow
  • User-Centric Design – Creating intuitive experiences for nurses fitting this in between patients, often on a shared phone and a weak connection
  • Multilingual Support – Building inclusive, accessible interfaces across regional languages

Overall Learnings:

  • Human-centered design is essential, focusing on ease of use, minimal cognitive load, and clear workflows for frontline healthcare workers
  • Technology adoption varies; gradual onboarding, guided interactions, and offline solutions are necessary
  • Multilingual UI design needs cultural context, readability, and layout adjustments beyond simple translation
