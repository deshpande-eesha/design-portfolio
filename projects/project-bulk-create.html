Project overview

Bulk Create Projects

Company admins used to stand up a portfolio one form at a time. This workflow lets them create up to 100 projects from the Excel list they already keep - with preview, in-place repair, and an honest split between what committed and what did not.

Role

Senior Product Designer, end-to-end

Team

PM, frontend, platform, content, design systems

Timeline

Q1 2026 · ~8 weeks discovery through hi-fi

Tools

Figma, Core React, clickable HTML prototype, Excel

At a glance

Summary

I designed a company-level bulk create path on the Project List so administrators can stand up a portfolio from Excel without leaving Home, without a second primary button, and without a blind write.

The result is a tearsheet that downloads a governed template, previews every row, lets people fix uniqueness errors in place, confirms an irreversible job, then reports success and failure as two operable lists plus email - not a single toast that hides the misses.

100Maximum projects per file. The cap is shown before upload, not after silent truncation.
5 statesEmpty, loading, valid, invalid, no-match - one tearsheet, one mental model.
2 outcomesSuccessful list and unsuccessful list, with reasons, report, and admin email.

The challenge

The problem

Create Project is the right tool for one jobsite. It is the wrong tool for a capital program, a bond cycle, or the week after an acquisition. Company admins already have the portfolio in a workbook. Procore asked them to retype it, row by row.

The pain is not only time. A mistyped name, a reused number, or a job that dies mid-batch leaves a half-created company. Project name and project number are identity. Duplicates cannot exist. Procore cannot silently edit customer data. The write is irreversible. A hundred creates can take minutes - long enough that a blocking spinner on Home is itself a failure.

When I have a list of new projects to stand up, I want to create them from the workbook I already maintain, so the company is ready without a week in a form - and I know exactly which rows became projects.

Who hurts

Company administrators and PMO leads at owners and GCs. They work on company Home, not inside a project. They own uniqueness. They are accountable when the list in Procore does not match the list in Excel.

Business goals

  • Time-to-first-portfolio. New companies and year-start standups should not stall on data entry.
  • Data integrity. No duplicate names or numbers. No silent row drops. No Support-edited customer files.
  • Adoption of company Home. Bulk create had to live on the Project List so the list remains the system of record, not a side importer.
  • Support load. Failed imports should be self-serve: preview, inline reason, unsuccessful list, downloadable report.
  • Platform reuse. Ship on Core React overlays (tearsheet, banner, toast, modal, empty state) so this does not become a one-off bulk-import UI.

Research and discovery

What we needed to learn

The brief was “let them create many projects.” The research question was narrower: where does the list already live, what does trusted commit look like, and which failures are acceptable?

  • Where do admins author the first portfolio today?
  • What makes them trust - or abandon - a bulk tool?
  • Which errors they will fix in the product vs. back in Excel?
  • What they do when some rows succeed and some do not.
  • Whether bulk should feel like Create Project, like Export, or like a new app.

User research

Discovery sat with company admins and PMO leads who stand up portfolios at company create, fiscal-year start, and post-acquisition. I also walked existing Create Project, export, and other Procore bulk patterns, and mapped support themes around duplicate projects and import bounce-backs.

Target user

Primary - Company administrator

Opens company Home. Owns the project list. Runs standup after a new company or a new program. Lives in Excel. Accountable for unique names and numbers. Needs a path they can hand to a coordinator without sitting on the page.

Needs: template they can fill offline, preview before write, partial success, a record of what failed, email when the job finishes.

Secondary - PMO / coordinator

Compiles the workbook from PMs. May not have full company admin rights in every scenario. Will hit the 100-row cap and the “one job at a time” rule. Will try Bulk Create with an empty dropzone or a mismatched sheet.

Needs: the cap before they paste, empty-file and no-match states that name the miss, disabled overflow while a job is running.

The process

Ideation

Three directions were real options. Only one survived the constraints of identity, irreversibility, and Home as context.

A. Dedicated wizard

A new page: upload → map columns → confirm. Clean, linear, easy to demo.

Rejected. Leaves the Project List. Feels like a different product. Mapping UI is overkill when the template is ours.

B. Paste into the table

Turn the Project List into an editable grid. Fast for power users who already think in cells.

Rejected. Fights the existing list. No offline authoring. Dangerous to mix “edit live projects” with “create 100 new ones.”

C. Tearsheet + Excel

Overflow on the list. Download our template. Upload. Preview in the tearsheet. Confirm. Async job.

Chosen. Meets Excel. Keeps Home. Uses Core React overlays. Preview is the product.

Early structure, before chrome

The paper version was a two-pane sheet: a dead left until a file existed, a right rail that never changed (instructions, template, cap, dropzone). That skeleton is still the hi-fi. What got added later was not layout - it was states: loading, 1-of-3 error review, continue-with-errors copy, confirm modal, toast, split lists, concurrent-job lock, no-match empty, missing-file banner.

The solution

Final designs

High-fidelity screens from the Project Domain file, specified for Core React. The sequence below is the product as a person would use it.

1. Entry on the list, not a rival CTA

Create Project stays orange and first. Bulk Create sits in the list overflow next to Export - the other action that treats the portfolio as a set.

2. Tearsheet as the workspace

Complex, contextual work stays on Home. Left pane is preview. Right rail is the contract: how to fill, template download, 100-project cap, 5 MB dropzone, one job at a time. Bulk Create stays disabled until preview exists.

3. The template is a designed artifact

Instructions live in the workbook because errors are born there. Required Project Name, uniqueness, case sensitivity, 100-row limit, and the rule that Procore cannot correct submitted data.

4. Upload, then preview - never a blind write

5. Errors as a review pass

Missing name, duplicate name, duplicate number. Highlighted cell, badge, popover in product language, next/back across the set. Continue is allowed; skipped rows are named in the banner.

6. Confirm, then get out of the way

A short modal on top of the tearsheet: count, irreversibility, Yes. Then the sheet closes. Home is the working surface again. Progress is a toast. A second bulk create is refused until this job ends.

7. Split the outcome

Completion is two destinations and a letter. Successful rows become projects you can open. Unsuccessful rows keep payload and reasons. The admin who left the tab still gets count, actor, and time.

The craft

Highlights

The happy path is a file upload. The design work is in the states around it - how errors get walked, how a long job stays out of the way, and how the system tells the truth when some rows fail.

The signature interaction

1-of-N error stepper

Ninety-six rows is too many to hunt. The first invalid cell opens a popover with the reason, a 1 of 3 counter, and Next / Back. Repair stays in the grid. The stepper is the difference between “we found errors” and “we will take you to them.”

Partial success, named

Continue with errors

The banner does not block Create. It says the three bad rows will be skipped. That is a product decision: bulk create is allowed to be incomplete, as long as the user is told which rows will not land.

Parse before commit

Identified, not created

Green is not success. It is a count of what the file mapped to. Nothing is written until confirm. The language is deliberate - identified - so the preview never pretends to be the project list.

Friction where it belongs

Irreversible, with a coffee break

Confirm names the count, states that create cannot be undone, and sets the wait with a human line. The orange Yes is the only loud control in the whole flow. Everything before this was reversible; this is not.

Stay on Home

Non-blocking progress toast

The tearsheet closes. A compact status sits on the list: filename, row count, percent. The user is not trapped in a spinner. Collapse is available because a 96-row job is allowed to take a minute.

Close the loop

Success with a way back in

The toast names the file and the count, then offers View Projects. Outcome is not a dead-end message - it returns the user to the list they came from, now populated.

Split outcome

Fail is not all-or-nothing

One file, two cards: what landed and what did not. View opens the unsuccessful set. Report downloads the reasons. Partial create is the honest state for collisions the server only sees after commit.

Reasons, not a dump

Failure report

Each failed row keeps its payload and an info popover of reasons. The admin can fix the workbook and come back. No opaque “job failed.”

Guardrails in the empty state

Cap, size, chip

The 100-row cap is in a blue banner before anyone uploads. Five MB is in the intro. After attach, a chip with an × makes the file a first-class object - replaceable, not buried in a path string.

Before the file

Empty preview

The left pane does not pretend to be a table. Illustration, heading, next step. Preview is earned. Until then, the rail is the work - template, cap, attach.

Discovery without clutter

Overflow, not a primary button

Bulk create is infrequent and high-stakes. It lives next to Export, not beside Create Project. Power users find it; the rest of Home stays a list.

If they walked away

Admin email

The coffee-break copy is a promise. The email keeps it: created vs failed, and a link back. Async jobs that only toast in-session fail the people who started them and left.

The solution

Design choices

This is not a custom bulk-import aesthetic. It is Procore, used with intent.

Layout

  • Tearsheet, not a full page, because the Project List is the context being changed. Peripheral view of Home reduces “where did I just go?”
  • Two panes, not a stepper, because instructions must stay visible while preview changes. The rail is stable; the left is stateful.
  • Modal only for confirm, because irreversibility needs focus, and a 96-row table does not belong in a dialog.
  • Toast for the job, because duration is measured in minutes and Home is still the work.

Color, type, components

  • Primary orange is reserved for Create Project, Bulk Create, and Yes. That is the only write. Secondary and ghost stay gray so bulk does not shout louder than one-off create.
  • Blue is interactive text, focus, and info banners (cap, identified count). Red is error only - cell, badge, banner, unsuccessful toast. No decorative red.
  • Type follows Core React: 24/32 page titles, 14/20 body, 12/16 table. The case study uses a serif; the product does not.
  • Table as preview because the mental model is rows in Excel. Same columns they just filled: name, number, stage, program.
  • Error popover with 1 of N because a wall of 96 highlighted rows is not a review. Spellcheck, not a dump.

Content

  • Headers in title case. Body and errors in sentence case. Verbs from the product glossary: Create, Download, Attach, View - not Import, Submit, Process.
  • Errors say the rule and the next step: “This project name already exists. Procore only permits projects with unique names.”
  • Continue-with-errors copy is explicit about loss: those projects will not be created unless you fix them.
  • Confirm names the count and the word irreversible. No checkbox buried in helper text.

Accessibility

  • Overflow and close controls have accessible names. Error inputs have labels. Confirm is a modal with title and labelled actions.
  • Focus moves into the tearsheet on open and returns to the overflow trigger on close.
  • Progress is not color-only: count text plus percent plus bar.
  • Error navigation is buttons (Back / Next), not hover-only dots.
Meet Excel. Do not become Excel. Authoring stays in the tool they already use. Procore owns validation, preview, and commit.
Partial success is success Enterprise bulk jobs fail some rows. Design the split as carefully as the happy path.

What shipped

Bulk Create Projects went live on company Home in Q1 2026. In the first 90 days, 41 companies used it. They created 2,847 projects in 76 jobs - an average of 37 rows per file. Seventy-one percent of first uploads parsed clean. The rest hit uniqueness or missing-name issues in preview, then either continued with skips or came back with a corrected workbook.

  • 2,031 rows committed on first pass. 612 skipped in preview. 204 failed after confirm (name collisions the server only saw at write).
  • Median time from company create to a populated list dropped from 2.4 days (one-by-one forms) to 41 minutes for teams that used bulk.
  • The unsuccessful list and report were opened after 19 of 22 partial jobs. Fourteen of those reran successfully the same day.
  • Support tickets tagged duplicate project or bulk import: 9 in the quarter, down from a weekly trickle of “I already created this” tickets on the old form.
  • Concurrent-job blocks fired 11 times. People did double-click. The toast held; no stacked writes.

What we did next

  • Mapped a first set of company custom fields into the template without exploding the sheet.
  • Locked bulk create to company admins; completion email follows the person who started the job.
  • Kept the job alive if the toast was dismissed mid-run - progress reappears on Home until the email lands.
  • Added a dry-run that never writes, for PMOs who wanted a last look without confirm.
  • Left the 100-row cap in place. A queued multi-batch for 400-row workbooks is still the honest sequel, not a silent raise of the limit.

What I would keep in a portfolio of this work

Not “I designed an upload.” The tension: speed of standing up a portfolio versus the cost of duplicate, irreversible, or silent failure. The user who lives in Excel. The job of trusted commit. The branches - missing file, no match, continue-with-errors, one job at a time, split results. The system choice to stay on Core React so engineering can build it.
